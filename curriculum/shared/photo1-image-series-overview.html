THE PROJECT / OVERVIEW
Capture a Series That Belongs Together
Module Overview
On this photo walk you use a classroom camera kit to capture a cohesive series: a group of photos that feel linked, like a family of images. Your series can be circles, shapes, colors, textures, reflections, or your own idea. What matters is that the photos clearly go together.
You capture in RAW, offload your photos to OneDrive, and import them into Lightroom Classic. You build your first contact sheet, then cull to your best 6, do a light edit, and turn in a final contact sheet.

THE CONCEPT / WHAT MAKES A SERIES
A Family of Images
Capturing a natural-light series on campus.
A series is more than a pile of photos. The images share something: the same subject, the same shapes, the same colors, or the same feeling. When someone looks at all of them together, they can tell the photos belong to each other.
• Pick one idea: circles, shapes, colors, textures, reflections, or your own theme.
• Keep it consistent: repeat that idea across every photo so they feel linked.
• Think like a set: each photo is part of a group, not a one-off.
HOW IT WORKS / YOUR PLAN
Your Three Steps
1Capture & Import: set the camera to RAW, capture your cohesive series, offload to OneDrive, import into Lightroom Classic, and turn in a 12-image contact sheet.
2Cull & Edit: pick your best 6, do a light edit (exposure, highlights, shadows, color temperature), and turn in a 6-image contact sheet.
3Reflection: tell the story of your series.
Capture in RAW, not JPG. Set your Canon EOS R50 to RAW before you start. Step 01 shows you how.
Capture your photos on purpose for this project. They must be new images you take on this walk, not pictures already in your camera roll from before.

EL CONCEPTO / QUÉ HACE UNA SERIE
Una Familia de Imágenes
Capturando una serie con luz natural en el campus.
Una serie es más que un montón de fotos. Las imágenes comparten algo: el mismo tema, las mismas formas, los mismos colores o la misma sensación. Cuando alguien las ve todas juntas, puede notar que las fotos van una con otra.
• Elige una idea: círculos, formas, colores, texturas, reflejos o tu propio tema.
• Manténla consistente: repite esa idea en cada foto para que se sientan unidas.
• Piensa como un grupo: cada foto es parte de un conjunto, no una foto suelta.
