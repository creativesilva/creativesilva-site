GEOMETRY, ALIGNMENT & CONSTRUCTION LOCK

Geometric accuracy is a major priority.

Do not rush the architectural construction of the room.

Prioritize structural accuracy, alignment, believable perspective, and clean geometry over speed, decorative detail, or unnecessary complexity.

Establish the classroom as a coherent architectural space before resolving small surface details.

The classroom has a clearly defined FRONT teaching wall.

Unless the current scene explicitly specifies a different arrangement:
- all student desks face the front of the classroom
- all computer monitors face the front of the classroom
- desk rows run parallel to one another
- desks within each row share consistent orientation
- corresponding desks align cleanly across rows
- workstation spacing follows a deliberate architectural grid
- aisles remain straight, intentional, and consistently spaced

Desks must NOT appear:
- randomly rotated
- slightly crooked
- scattered
- misaligned
- unintentionally staggered
- warped
- twisted
- floating
- intersecting other furniture
- facing random directions

Maintain consistent desk dimensions throughout the classroom.

All equivalent desks should use:
- the same tabletop width
- the same tabletop depth
- the same tabletop thickness
- the same leg design
- the same leg spacing
- the same desk height
- the same structural construction

Perspective may make distant desks appear smaller, but their actual physical dimensions remain identical.

Furniture alignment must obey the perspective of the requested camera position.

Parallel desk edges should converge naturally toward the correct vanishing point when perspective requires it.

Do not independently distort individual desks to create depth.

Straight architectural elements must remain straight in 3D space:
- desk edges
- wall seams
- window mullions
- ceiling panels
- linear lights
- door frames
- floor joints
- architectural trim

These lines may converge naturally through perspective but must not bend, bow, ripple, kink, or warp.

Maintain a coherent shared perspective system across the entire image.

All desks, walls, windows, ceiling lines, floor lines, and architectural structures must agree with the same camera position and vanishing-point geometry.

Do not create conflicting perspective systems in different parts of the image.

Desk legs must remain vertical and structurally plausible unless perspective naturally changes their projected angle.

Each desk must make believable floor contact.

No desk legs may:
- merge into neighboring desks
- disappear without logical occlusion
- multiply
- bend unnaturally
- terminate above the floor
- pass through objects
- change construction from desk to desk

Chairs should occupy logical positions relative to their assigned workstation.

Chairs may rotate naturally based on the person using them, but the DESKS themselves remain aligned with the classroom layout unless explicitly directed otherwise.

Camera freedom does NOT mean furniture-layout randomness.

The virtual camera may move freely around the room while the underlying classroom remains organized, aligned, and architecturally coherent.

CHAIRS — HARD GEOMETRY LOCK

The chairs are a critical identity feature.

- Modern ergonomic task chair.
- Absolutely no armrests.
- Open lightweight silhouette.
- Brushed or matte aluminum perimeter frame.
- Fine translucent charcoal-gray mesh back.
- Mesh must remain visibly permeable.
- When a person is seated, their torso, waist, clothing, and body silhouette must remain perceptible through the mesh.
- Do not render the back as opaque fabric, solid plastic, or a solid black panel.
- Black or deep-charcoal upholstered seat cushion.
- Realistic central gas-lift column.
- Exactly five-point base.
- Exactly five base arms.
- Exactly five caster positions.
- Never six.
- Base is brushed or matte aluminum, not mirror chrome.
- Black dual-wheel casters with correct floor contact.
- Keep chair proportions and construction consistent throughout the room.
- Chairs may rotate naturally, but their design cannot change.

LIGHTING

Use a physically believable layered lighting system.

Natural daylight:
- Bright clean daylight when glazing is visible or nearby.
- Preserve exterior detail through windows.
- Avoid blown-out white windows.

Cool-white architectural ambient light:
- Long recessed linear ceiling fixtures.
- Soft wall washes.
- Restrained integrated downlights.
- Bright enough for a professional classroom without flattening all shadows.

Teal accent light:
- Core design hue: #007474.
- Use as a restrained architectural accent only.
- Natural emission bloom may appear slightly brighter or more cyan, but the underlying color identity remains #007474.
- Do not turn the room into a nightclub, gaming lab, or cyberpunk environment.

Lighting must produce:
- believable global illumination
- soft shadow falloff
- realistic contact shadows
- correct reflected light on metal
- subtle floor reflections
- realistic light interaction with mesh chair backs
- dimensionality without artificial HDR
